3D Nodes & Scene Entities

The Tessera 3D scene graph is built on an extensible object-oriented hierarchy. Every entity in the scene inherits from the base Node class (src/Scene/Node.h) and can be parented, transformed, animated, scripted, and replicated across the network.

ℹ Universal Node Properties

Every node possesses a unique 64-bit unsigned ID (node->id), a string identifier (node->name), parent ID (node->parentId), list of children (node->childrenIds), local position/rotation/scale, world transform caching, visibility flag, script file path, and a multiplayerAuthority peer ID.

1. Overview of 3D Scene Nodes

Node Class Tag Enum Type Primary Role & Usage
MeshNode [3D] NodeType::Mesh Renders 3D procedural geometric primitives or imported FBX/glTF/OBJ model meshes with full PBR shading.
CharacterNode [3D] NodeType::Character Specialized humanoid character NPC featuring waypoint patrol steering, dialogue interaction, animations, and ground snapping.
CameraNode [CAM] NodeType::Camera 3D perspective viewing camera with configurable Field of View, clipping planes, and picture-in-picture preview rendering.
LightNode [LGT] NodeType::Light Scene illumination source supporting Directional (Sun with CSM), Point (omnidirectional), Spot (focused cone), and Area (Rect) (rectangular LTC area light) lighting.
AudioNode [SND] NodeType::AudioSource Positional 3D spatial or 2D stereo sound emitter with distance rolloff curves, Doppler effect, and audio bus routing.
ParticleNode [VFX] NodeType::ParticleEmitter Particle effects (fire, smoke, sparks, rain, magic, heat haze) on the CPU or GPU: flipbooks, soft particles, trails and ribbons, mesh particles and collision.
DecalNode [DCL] NodeType::Decal Oriented Bounding Box (OBB) surface decal projector that projects textures and normal maps onto scene geometry.
SplineNode [SPL] NodeType::Spline 3D parametric Catmull-Rom and Bezier curve for rollercoasters, patrol routes, camera rails, and procedural extrusion.

TerrainNode [TRN] NodeType::Terrain Subdivided heightmap landscape with procedural generation (Rolling Hills, Mountain Peaks, Canyons & Ravines), 2D heightmap PNG import, and interactive real-time sculpting brushes.

2. MeshNode (3D Mesh & Primitives)

MeshNode (src/Scene/Nodes/3D/MeshNode.h) is the primary visual building block for static and dynamic 3D geometry. It can render either procedurally generated mathematical shapes or custom complex meshes loaded from 3D asset files.

Shape Types

  • Cube: Unit box with face normals, tangent vectors, and UV coordinates mapped per face.
  • Sphere: Lat/long tessellated UV sphere with smooth normal vectors.
  • Cylinder: Cylindrical tube with capped top and bottom discs.
  • Cone: Circular base tapering to a single apex vertex.
  • Plane: Flat horizontal quad suitable for ground, floors, and water surfaces.
  • Custom 3D Model Asset: Loads an external mesh asset (.fbx, .gltf, .glb, .obj) with vertex skinning, bone weights, materials, and LOD hierarchies.

In-Game Video Screens & Monitors

Any 3D mesh surface in Tessera can be turned into an active video screen. When isVideoScreen is enabled, the VideoSystem engine decodes external media or generates real-time procedural broadcast channels, uploading the dynamic frame buffer to Vulkan texture slot @video_<nodeId> without requiring scene recompilation or shader swaps.

  • Real-Time Screen Light Bounce: In-game screens act as dynamic light sources! Every frame, the screen's average HDR color and brightness are sampled and fed directly into the Real-Time Voxel Global Illumination (VXGI) and Screen-Space Reflections (SSR) pipelines, illuminating actors and room geometry around the screen with authentic color bounce.
  • Supported Formats: Animated GIFs (decoded via stbi_load_gif_from_memory), sprite-sheet video atlases with custom column/row counts, sequential numbered PNG frames, and procedural real-time TV channels.
  • Form-Factor Presets: Built-in presets automatically configure UV aspect ratios, scanlines, and content: Living Room TV Set (16:9), Cinema Projection Screen (2.39:1), Smartphone / Mobile Screen (9:16), CCTV Night-Vision Monitor (4:3), Retro CRT Color Bars (4:3), and Sci-Fi Radar Telemetry (1:1).

3. CharacterNode (Humanoid NPC)

CharacterNode (src/Scene/Nodes/3D/CharacterNode.h) inherits from MeshNode, providing high-level gameplay automation for interactive humanoid characters, quest givers, wandering civilians, and patrol guards.

Key Capabilities

  • Waypoint Patrol Engine: Automatically steers the character through a list of 3D world positions with configurable turn speed (yaw), move speed, and arrival tolerance.
  • Looping Patrols: When enabled, the character loops back to waypoint 0 upon reaching the final waypoint; otherwise, it stops and transitions to idle.
  • Ground Raycasting & Snapping: Fires a physical down-ray into the scene to snap the character's feet smoothly to slopes and uneven terrain.
  • Dialogue Prompt & Interaction Radius: Displays dialogue text prompts when the player enters the character's proximity trigger radius.
  • Automatic Animation State Binding: Links idle and walk skeletal animation clips so the character automatically plays walkAnimClip while navigating and switches to idleAnimClip when stationary.

4. CameraNode

CameraNode represents an in-game viewpoint. When active, it calculates the view matrix (glm::lookAt) and perspective projection matrix (glm::perspective) used by the renderer.

  • fov: Vertical Field of View in degrees (default 60°).
  • nearClip: Near depth clipping plane distance (default 0.1m).
  • farClip: Far depth clipping plane distance (default 1000.0m).
  • isPrimary: Designates this camera as the active rendering target for the game loop.
  • Scene Lifecycle & Deletion: Camera nodes can be freely created, deleted, or duplicated. Deleting a camera (via Del or the Inspector's Delete Node from Scene button) cleanly removes it from both the viewport and hierarchy without phantom auto-respawn loops. If a scene contains no active camera, the camera preview offscreen buffer cleanly clears while the main 3D viewport retains the editor spectator camera.

5. LightNode

LightNode illuminates scene geometry and casts dynamic shadows. Lights use clustered forward shading, so up to 1,024 lights per view can be active; the sun and up to 4 point or spot lights cast shadows at once. Three distinct illumination modes are supported:

  • Directional Light: Parallel light rays simulating distant sun or moon light. Includes a 4-split Cascaded Shadow Map (CSM) covering near, mid, and far distance bands.
  • Point Light: Radiates light spherically in all directions with inverse-square falloff that fades smoothly to zero at its range. Can cast cube-map shadows.
  • Spot Light: Conical spotlight with adjustable innerCutoffAngle and outerCutoffAngle for soft penumbra edges, ideal for flashlights, streetlights, and vehicle headlights.
  • Area Light (Rect): Rectangular area light (LightType::AreaRect) with configurable areaLightSize (width and height in meters) and areaLightTwoSided emission, evaluated on the GPU via Linearly Transformed Cosines (LTC) for broad, realistic window and studio softbox highlights.

6. AudioNode

AudioNode attaches an audio source to the scene graph. In 3D mode, the sound's volume and pan are computed based on the listener's camera position, with distance attenuation curves and Doppler frequency shifts based on node velocity.

7. ParticleNode

ParticleNode emits particles continuously or in bursts and moves them by gravity, drag, vortex, attractor and curl-noise forces, with size, colour and alpha over their life, rotation and spin. It runs on the CPU, or on the GPU in a compute shader (Simulation Backend, up to 1,000,000 particles).

  • Render Modes:
    • Billboard (Additive Glow): Camera-facing quads with additive blending and depth-write disabled. Overlapping particles accumulate radiance, creating realistic bright cores for fire, sparks, and energy blasts without solid depth clipping.
    • Billboard (Alpha Blend): Camera-facing quads with translucent alpha blending, depth-write disabled, and procedural soft feathering. Perfect for billowy campfire smoke, ground fog, and atmospheric mist.
    • 3D Mesh (Sphere / Cube): Instanced, lit spheres or cubes that tumble as they spin, for debris, shrapnel and voxel bursts (Self Glow for embers).
    • Distortion (Heat Haze): Bends the finished scene behind it with a rippling offset (Distortion sets how far).
  • 1-Click VFX Presets:
    • 🔥 Fire / Torch Flame: Upward-rising cone emission with hot golden-yellow core and red embers, curl noise turbulence, and additive glow.
    • 💨 Campfire Smoke: Expanding alpha-blended plume with soft edges, negative gravity, and low-frequency curl drift.
    • 🌫️ Ground Fog / Mist: Large-scale horizontal box emission with smooth radial falloff, low opacity, and subtle drift.
    • ✨ Magic Sparkles: Multi-colored swirling particles driven by vortex force modules and additive luminance.
    • 💥 Explosion Burst: A burst of the built-in explosion sheet: fireballs that grow, cool through orange to dark smoke and thin away, the fire glowing (Self Glow).
    • Sparks: Fast stretched sparks with trails that bounce off the scene.
    • Heat Haze: Rising distortion for above fires and exhausts.
    • Magic Ribbon: A glowing ribbon through the particles in birth order: move the emitter to draw a swoosh.
    • Rain Splashes: Drops that die where they land and throw up small splashes.
  • Look: a texture played as a sprite sheet (Columns, Rows, Frames, over life or at a fixed rate, random start, blended frames), or a Built-in Sheet (Fire, Smoke, Explosion, Sparks) without one; Softness (soft particles), Camera Fade, Velocity Stretch, Lit, Glow Intensity, Self Glow.
  • Trails & Ribbons: Per Particle trails (Points, Point Interval, Width, Taper) or one Ribbon through the particles; Draw Particles Too, Texture Tiling.
  • Collision: Ground Plane (a height) or Scene (meshes, terrain, and water with Sink Into Water); Bounce, Friction, Life Lost per Hit, Die On Hit, Spawn on Hit (splashes and sparks, with speed and size).
  • Lua: self:EmitParticles(n), self:SetEmitting(on), self:GetParticleCount(), self:GetParticleHits(), self:ResetParticles().

8. DecalNode

DecalNode projects textures onto existing static and dynamic 3D meshes without modifying geometry. Ideal for bullet impact holes, blood splatters, tire tracks, and road markings. Projected within an Oriented Bounding Box (OBB) with normal angle rejection.

The surface under each pixel is rebuilt from the view's depth prepass and textured where it faces the box's y axis within the Normal Angle Cutoff, fading at the box's ends and beyond the Fade Distance. Blend Mode: Alpha Blend paints a lit layer (with the node's normal map), Stain / Multiply darkens the surface, Emissive / Additive glows in the node's emissive colour. Overlapping decals draw in Sort Priority order.

9. SplineNode

SplineNode creates continuous 3D mathematical curves passing through an arbitrary number of control points. Supports Catmull-Rom interpolation for automatic tangent smoothing. Used for camera tracks, train rails, and character movement paths.

10. ColliderNode

ColliderNode specifies collision boundaries for JoltPhysics. Available in Box (extents X, Y, Z), Sphere (radius), and Capsule (radius, height) shapes. Can be set as physical solid obstacles or non-physical trigger volumes for gameplay events.

11. EmptyNode

EmptyNode carries no mesh or renderer payload. It serves as a pure spatial transform node for grouping related entities, organizing levels, or acting as rotational pivot points (e.g., turret bases or orbiting camera mounts).

12. TerrainNode

TerrainNode generates dynamic 3D landscapes with customizable resolution (up to 256×256 vertices), arbitrary world dimensions, and continuous heightfield sampling for character navigation.

  • Procedural Generators:
    • Rolling Hills: Multi-octave continuous harmonic noise for countryside, mounds, and meadows.
    • Mountain Peaks: Ridged multifractal peaks and alpine ridge crests reaching dramatic altitudes.
    • Canyons & Ravines: High elevated plateaus carved through by winding river chasms and deep gorges.
    • Flatten: Resets the terrain to a flat ground surface.
  • Brush: tick Brush Active, pick a tool (Sculpt, Paint Layers, Place Foliage, Erase Foliage) and drag over the terrain; the brush follows the ground under the cursor.
  • Paint layers: up to four (texture or plain tint, tiling in metres, roughness), blended by a painted splat map with height blending at their edges. Auto Paint from Slope & Height fills the map from the land's shape.
  • Foliage: built-in grass, flowers, bushes, broadleaf trees, pines and rocks, or any model, scattered at each type's density and slope limit; they follow the ground, sway in the wind, cast shadows and can be solid in Play.
  • Physics: in Play the terrain is a solid heightfield.
  • Interactive 3D Sculpting Tools:
    • Raise: Pulls land upward to create mounds, hills, and cliffs.
    • Lower: Carves valleys, ravines, craters, and riverbeds.
    • Smooth: Softens sharp vertices using local neighbor averaging.
    • Flatten: Levels land to a specified plateau height.
    • Click and drag directly in the 3D viewport with a visible green brush circle to sculpt in real time!
  • Heightmap Import: Load any grayscale .png or .jpg heightmap image to instantly recreate real-world GIS topography or digital elevation models (DEM).
  • Continuous Height Sampling: Characters, NPCs, vehicles, and physics raycasts can call SampleHeight(worldX, worldZ) for exact bilinear-interpolated ground elevation.
