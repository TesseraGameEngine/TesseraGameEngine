Audio Engine, Spatial Audio & DSP Mixer

The Tessera Audio System (src/Audio/) provides high-performance multi-channel audio mixing, 3D spatial sound attenuation, Doppler frequency calculation, sub-bus routing, and real-time Digital Signal Processing (DSP) acoustic effects.

1. 3D Spatial Audio & Attenuation

When an AudioNode is placed in the scene, its sound is spatialized relative to the primary CameraNode (the listener):

  • Listener Orientation: Stereo panning and interaural time difference (ITD) calculated from camera forward/up vectors.
  • Distance Attenuation Curves:
    • Inverse Distance: Natural sound falloff inversely proportional to distance.
    • Linear Rolloff: Volume decreases uniformly between minDistance and maxDistance.
    • Exponential Rolloff: Rapid steep drop-off for intimate, localized sound effects.
  • Doppler Frequency Shift: Pitch scales dynamically based on the relative velocity between moving audio emitters (e.g., speeding vehicles, whistling arrows) and the listener.

3. Real-Time DSP Audio Effects

  • Environmental Reverb: Simulates acoustic reflections in enclosed spaces. Parameters: Room Size, Damping, Early Reflection Gain, Wet/Dry Mix.
  • Chorus: Multi-voice detuned delay lines creating rich, shimmering acoustic depth.
  • Distortion / Saturation: Overdrive clipping for explosions, crunchy radio transmissions, and sci-fi lasers.
  • Resonant Filters: Low-Pass (muffles sound underwater or behind thick walls) and High-Pass filters with adjustable resonance Q.
  • Pitch Shifting: Changes audio frequency without altering playback speed.

4. 3D Reverb Zones

Reverb zones are 3D trigger volumes in your level (caves, cathedrals, sewers, open fields). When the player walks into a Reverb Zone, the engine smoothly crossfades the DSP reverb parameters to match the physical environment seamlessly.

5. Ray-Traced Acoustic Diffraction & Door Portaling (AAA Standard)

To deliver realistic environmental acoustics, Tessera integrates a geometric acoustic ray tracer (Audio::AcousticRayTracer) that models true wave propagation and diffraction around physical obstacles:

Physical Wall Transmission Loss

When line-of-sight between an audio emitter and the listener is occluded by level geometry, the engine computes transmission loss based on physical wall materials:

  • Concrete: 35 dB attenuation with heavy high-frequency absorption (≤ 450 Hz cutoff).
  • Wood: 15 dB attenuation with warm acoustic resonance (≤ 1200 Hz cutoff).
  • Glass: 22 dB attenuation with bright acoustic reflection (≤ 800 Hz cutoff).
  • Curtain / Fabric: 6 dB attenuation allowing sound penetration with mild dampening.

Huygens' Door & Window Portaling

When an open portal (doorway, window, hallway) connects two rooms, sound diffracts through the opening following Huygens' Principle:

  • Virtual Sound Source: The listener perceives sound originating from the doorway opening rather than directly through solid walls.
  • Dynamic Aperture Modulation: As doors open or close (0.0 to 1.0 aperture), high frequencies are smoothly filtered and directional attenuation adjusts in real time.
